Krea launched Ad Studio on October 29, and if the idea sounds familiar, it should. Turning a website link into a set of ads has become a crowded category this fall. What's worth a look here is how much of the process Krea hands back to you before it generates anything.

## What changed

Ad Studio is the first of what Krea calls Experts, versions of its Krea 3 model built for one specific job. Here's the flow Krea describes:

- **Paste a URL.** It researches the site and builds a brand kit with the logo, colors, fonts, a description, tone of voice, audience, and selling points. It also looks for products with photos and prices, and it reads the product catalog on Shopify stores. A kit holds up to 20 products, and you can add them by link, by upload, or by hand. If there's no website, you can set up the brand by hand.
- **Edit the kit.** You can change anything it picked up before you generate. Krea's point is that the ads follow the kit, so the claims on them are the ones you approved.
- **Pick placements and products.** Instagram feed gets 4:5 and 1:1, Stories and Reels get 9:16, and landscape feed gets 16:9. You choose how many ads to make for each product, up to 12 in one batch, and each ad features one product with its own layout and copy.
- **Review and refine.** The ads open in a gallery next to Krea Agent, where you can ask for a new headline, a darker background, another product, or another size. You can preview an ad in a Facebook, Instagram, or Reels mockup, then download one ad or the whole campaign as a ZIP.

Krea's own timed run on one real store had the kit ready in 49 seconds and six Story ads done in 2 minutes 28 seconds, with about five minutes from pasting the URL to downloading. That's one run by the company selling it, so treat it as a best guess rather than a benchmark.

Ad Studio also works through Krea's MCP server, so you can ask Claude to research a brand, pick products, and generate ads there.

## Why it matters for designers

The editable kit is the part to pay attention to. A scraped brand is only as good as the site it came from, and plenty of sites are full of expired promos and old positioning. Stopping to fix that before generating is the same review a designer would want to do anyway, and it's a better place to catch problems than 12 finished ads later.

This kind of tool seems aimed mostly at people who don't have a designer, but it's still useful to know about if you do this work. It could be a quick way to rough out directions for a small ecommerce client, or to fill out product variations once the look has been settled. It's also something a client may show up having already tried, so it's worth knowing what it can and can't do.

## What to watch out for

We haven't tried this, and everything above comes from Krea's own post, including the claim that ads stick to the kit. Read every ad before it goes out. Krea says it pulls prices and product photos from the site, but it doesn't say anything about checking them, so a stale price could end up in an ad.

A brand kit built from a website also isn't the same as brand guidelines. It can pick up a logo and colors, but Krea doesn't describe any way to feed in the rules on how they should be used, so don't expect it to know your clear space or color ratios.

Ad Studio only makes static ads, and only one product per ad, so bundle shots and multi-product layouts aren't part of this as described.

The data and rights questions matter more than usual here, because you're pointing it at someone's whole brand. The sidebar has what Krea's terms do and don't say. The short version is that they're silent on AI training outside the Business plan, so ask before you run an unannounced product through it.
