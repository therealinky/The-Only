Apple announced a big round of updates to its Creator Studio apps on October 27, arriving in the App Store in November. There's a lot in it, from Logic Pro to Freeform, but three pieces stand out for designers: a generative fill in Pixelmator Pro, MotionVFX built into Final Cut Pro, and agents reaching into Keynote.

## What changed

- **Generate Fill in Pixelmator Pro.** Circle part of an image, or use the Free, Quick, or Magnetic Selection tools for something more precise, then describe what you want there. Apple says it matches the lighting, perspective, and texture around it, and uses Super Resolution to upscale what it generates. Results can merge into an existing layer or go on a new layer with a mask, so you can keep refining.
- **MotionVFX inside Final Cut Pro.** What used to be a third-party add-on is now built in, with what Apple calls thousands of elements, including templates, plus rotoscoping, tracking, and color grading tools. You can also open a timeline image in Pixelmator Pro and have the edit save back to your project.
- **Motion gets text-to-image** with round-trip editing in Pixelmator Pro, along with built-in MotionVFX plug-ins for color grading, 3D camera tracking, and one-click selection.
- **Assistants in Keynote, Pages, and Numbers (beta).** In Keynote, Apple says the assistant can help design, animate, and refine slides. It's built on Apple models and OpenAI, needs an Apple Intelligence capable device on the new 27 releases, and is U.S. English only.
- **MCP support for Keynote, Pages, and Numbers (Mac only).** Apple says outside AI agents and tools can read content, make edits, and run automated actions in these apps. It needs macOS 27. Keynote also adds support for an open, JSON-based Keynote Interchange Format (.keyx).

## Why it matters for designers

Generate Fill isn't new as an idea, but having it in Pixelmator Pro matters if that's the editor you picked to stay out of a subscription. The layer and mask handling is the detail to like here. A fill that lands on its own masked layer is something you can fix, not something you have to accept.

The MCP piece is the sleeper. Plenty of designers live in Keynote for pitch decks and client presentations, and an agent that can read and edit those files could take on the tedious parts, like rebuilding a deck in a new template or updating numbers across slides. Pair that with a JSON-based file format and decks start to look like something you can generate and check programmatically.

For editors, folding MotionVFX into Final Cut Pro removes a separate purchase and a separate install for a lot of common motion graphics work.

## What to watch out for

None of this has shipped yet. Everything here is from Apple's own announcement, and we haven't tried any of it. Several features are labeled beta, and the assistants are U.S. English only.

Apple didn't say whether Generate Fill runs on device or in the cloud, or what model powers it. For a company that usually leads with privacy, that's a notable gap, and it's worth finding out before you use it on client photos.

Be careful with MCP. Giving an outside agent edit access to your documents means its mistakes land in your files, and its data terms now apply to whatever it reads. Work on copies until you trust the setup, and check the sidebar for what Apple has and hasn't confirmed.

Requirements are steep. The Keynote assistant needs the new 27 releases, and MCP needs macOS 27, so an older Mac or a delayed OS upgrade means waiting.
