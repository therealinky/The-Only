Setting up a Brand Kit in Canva has always meant manually uploading a logo, typing in hex codes, and picking fonts one at a time. As of Canva's September 2026 feature update, you can skip most of that: point Brand Kit Builder at a company website or a brand guidelines PDF, and it fills the kit in for you.

## What changed

Per Canva's own help documentation, Brand Kit Builder "automatically creates a Brand Kit using content extracted from a website or PDF." From a website, it can extract or generate logos, colors, fonts, graphics and other visual assets, photography, brand voice, and guidelines. From a PDF brand guidelines document specifically, it pulls brand colors, font styles, brand voice, and guidelines.

It's built into three different entry points: starting a brand-new or empty Brand Kit, going through team onboarding, or filling gaps in an existing Brand Kit that's missing key elements (Canva surfaces a banner prompting you to run the builder in that last case). Website extraction is close to instant, according to Canva; PDF extraction takes roughly a minute on average, more for large or complex files. Canva's own documentation is upfront that results vary by source: results may be sparser for a poorly structured site or a scanned, non-machine-readable PDF, and it recommends reviewing everything the tool pulls in before you rely on it.

## Why it matters for designers

Brand Kit setup is exactly the kind of task that's necessary but rarely gets done properly, especially for smaller teams or freelancers taking on a new client where nobody wants to spend an hour manually re-entering hex codes and re-uploading logo variants that already exist somewhere. If this reliably pulls a reasonable first draft from an existing site or PDF, it turns Brand Kit setup from a chore into a five-minute review-and-correct pass, which matters most exactly when you're onboarding a new client or team fast.

It's also a meaningful lower bar for actually using Brand Kits and Brand Controls at all. A tool that only helps once someone's already done the manual setup work doesn't help the teams who never get around to that setup in the first place.

## What to watch out for

This is explicitly a starting point, not a finished Brand Kit. Canva's own documentation says results depend heavily on the source material's quality and structure, and recommends reviewing every extracted asset, color, and guideline before treating the kit as authoritative, particularly brand voice and written guidelines, which are the most likely to come out generic or slightly off from a website scrape.

It's also gated by plan and role: Canva Pro, Teams, Enterprise, Education, or Nonprofit, and only for team owners, admins, or brand designers with Brand Kit edit access, not something every team member can run for themselves. And if a Brand Kit already has its key elements filled in, the builder won't offer to replace them, so it's aimed at new setup and gap-filling, not re-auditing an established kit.
