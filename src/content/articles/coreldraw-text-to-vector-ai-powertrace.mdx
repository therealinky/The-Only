CorelDRAW has joined the text-to-vector crowd. Its September 2026 update, announced on September 15, adds a generator that produces editable vector artwork from a written prompt or from artwork you already have, plus a noticeably faster version of its PowerTRACE bitmap tracer.

## What changed

**Text-to-vector.** Describe what you want, or start from existing artwork, and CorelDRAW generates vector graphics you can then edit like anything else in the document. Corel names two models behind it, Vector Craft and Vector Master. The pitch is scalable output that works from a web graphic up to a billboard.

**PowerTRACE, faster and tidier.** Corel says bitmap-to-vector tracing is up to four times faster, with better object structure, better color handling, and AI background removal built into the trace step.

**CorelDRAW Web catches up.** The browser version can now use system, brand, and custom fonts, and export DWG and DXF directly. That second one matters for anyone sending work to plotters, vinyl cutters, or laser and CNC fabrication.

## Why it matters for designers

The honest test for any text-to-vector tool is the cleanup afterward. A raster generator gives you something to look at; a vector generator is only useful if the paths it gives you are sane enough to edit, recolor, and hand to a printer. Generating inside the same app where you'd do that cleanup is the real advantage here, since there's no import step and the result lands in your document ready to adjust.

The PowerTRACE update is arguably the more useful one day to day. Tracing a client's low-res logo is a job a lot of designers still do regularly, and faster, cleaner traces with background removal save real time.

## What to watch out for

Be careful with brand marks. We couldn't find anything from Corel on what the models were trained on or what rights come with the output, and that matters a lot more for a logo than for a spot illustration. Until that's clear, use it for exploration rather than final identity work.

Check the path count. AI-generated and auto-traced vectors often come out with far more nodes than a hand-drawn version, which can cause trouble in cutting and fabrication workflows, the same people the new DWG/DXF export is aimed at.

The free "unlimited generation" promotion ended on September 22, and Corel's own materials don't spell out what the credit allowance looks like after that, so check the current pricing yourself before counting on a specific number. We're going off Corel's announcement and haven't tried the update ourselves, so treat the sidebar as unverified.
