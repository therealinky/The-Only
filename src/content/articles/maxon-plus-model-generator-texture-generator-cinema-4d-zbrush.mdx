Maxon has launched Maxon Plus, a new subscription that adds cloud-based AI generation to Cinema 4D and ZBrush. Type a prompt or drop in a reference image, and it builds an editable 3D model. Describe a surface, and it textures a model you already have. It also comes with a browser-based review tool for sharing work with clients.

The important framing is that this is all opt-in and paid on top of what you already have. Maxon Plus isn't part of Maxon One, and Maxon has added a switch that turns AI off across your whole account.

## What changed

Maxon Plus launches with four services:

- **Model Generator** turns a prompt or reference image into an editable 3D model, on the web and inside Cinema 4D. Maxon says it needs Cinema 4D 2027.1 or later, and you pick which third-party AI provider generates the model. Maxon's FAQ names Tripo and Rodin as examples and handles those accounts itself, so you don't sign up with them separately. Creative Bloq reports that generation starts with Rodin only, with more providers planned.
- **Texture Generator** creates a texture for an existing model from a description of the surface you want or from reference images, on the web and in Cinema 4D.
- **Clay Builder** generates untextured base meshes to sculpt from in ZBrush, on the web and in ZBrush for iPad.
- **Maxon Review** lets you share 3D models, videos, and images in a browser so reviewers can comment without installing anything. It's a Public Preview at launch and doesn't use credits for now. Maxon says reviews between Maxon Plus subscribers will stay unlimited, but it will start using credits in other cases once it becomes a regular feature. You can't upload .c4d or .zbr files to it directly.

Cloud rendering is listed as coming soon, first for Cinema 4D on iPad.

Each Maxon Plus pack gives you a monthly allowance of credits: 3,000 for Starter at $10 a month, 9,000 for Creator at $25, and 25,000 for Pro at $60 in Maxon's US store, and the cloud AI steps spend them: concept preview images, multi-view image sets, final models, and texture passes. Packs stack, so you can combine them. Maxon's existing local AI tools, like Imagesense and Red Giant's Depth Generator, don't use credits.

Alongside it, Maxon introduced a separate **Maxon Agentic** license. It's a monthly subscription that lets an AI agent you bring yourself, such as Claude or ChatGPT, run its own background copy of Cinema 4D or Autograph through MCP while you keep working in yours. It isn't included in Maxon One or Maxon Plus.

## Why it matters for designers

For motion designers and 3D generalists, the slow part of a lot of jobs is getting a believable placeholder or base mesh on screen before the real work starts. A prompt-to-model step inside Cinema 4D, and a prompt-to-base-mesh step for ZBrush sculpting, aims squarely at that blocking-out stage. Maxon itself describes the Texture Generator as not replacing manual UV and texture cleanup for final production, which is a fair read of where this fits: early concepts, not finished assets.

The controls are worth noticing too, especially if you're skeptical. You can disable Maxon Plus to hide every cloud AI feature, or switch off AI entirely across your account, which Maxon says also covers its local AI tools and MCP integrations. Team admins can enforce that for everyone. That's a clearer off switch than many tools offer, with one limit: Maxon says it doesn't cover integrations you build yourself or custom scripts that connect to outside AI services.

## What to watch out for

The prices are clear, but what you get for them isn't. Maxon lists the packs from $10 a month, but it doesn't publish how many credits a typical model, texture, or preview set uses, so you won't know how far 3,000 credits go until you try. Since credits don't carry over and can't be refunded, and a job can't be cancelled once it starts, it's worth checking the cost Maxon shows before each run, especially for multi-view sets. Maxon does say credits come back if a job fails because of a system error on its side or the provider's.

Back in March, Maxon announced a partnership with Tencent Cloud to bring Tencent's HY 3D engine into Cinema 4D for prompt-to-model generation, with Cinema 4D for iPad first. The Maxon Plus pages name Tripo and Rodin as providers and don't mention Tencent, so it isn't clear yet whether HY 3D is one of the options. Either way, the models themselves come from third parties, not Maxon, so what they were trained on isn't something Maxon's own no-training promise covers.

Generated models also don't land as ordinary files. Maxon's FAQ says the .glb or .fbx files are saved encrypted on your computer so they go through Maxon's own optimization pipeline, so if you want to take a raw generated model into Blender or another tool, check how that works before you rely on it.

A few smaller limits: you need to be online to start a job, you have to be 18 or older to use the cloud AI tools, and team purchases aren't supported yet. The AI labels on generated models and textures can be stripped, so don't rely on them as proof of anything. And with Maxon Agentic, Maxon warns that a connected AI tool can read your whole project and send it to that provider under the provider's own terms, which may include training on free plans.
