Adobe has brought AI Blemish Removal to Lightroom on desktop. It's based on Adobe's announcement and one hands-on writeup, and we haven't tried it ourselves.

## What changed

Adobe's November 10 announcement describes Blemish Removal as "one-tap retouching with fine-tuning controls." Adobe says its new Creative Cloud capabilities are generally available, and it lists only a few other items as beta, which didn't include this one.

The more detailed picture comes from a photographer's writeup. It says the tool moved from Lightroom mobile to desktop, and that it detects blemishes for you. Separate sliders handle temporary marks and natural features such as moles and freckles, so you can clear a breakout and leave a freckle alone. Remove and Restore brushes refine the result, and in group shots you can adjust each face on its own.

That writeup doesn't say whether Lightroom Classic gets it, and neither source lists limitations.

## Why it matters for designers

Portrait and event photographers already know the slog of spot-healing every face in a set. A tool that finds blemishes first, then lets you decide how much of each kind to keep, shifts the work from clicking spots to judging the look.

The per-face adjustment is the part that sounds most useful for group shots, where one global setting rarely suits everyone.

## What to watch out for

Over-retouching is the obvious risk. Skin that's too smooth reads as fake, and the freckle and mole slider exists for a reason, so check at 100 percent.

Be careful about what counts as a blemish. Scars, birthmarks, and similar features can matter to the person wearing them, so make sure you're removing what the subject expects to lose.

And since the sources don't say how the tool is processed or priced, check both before you use it on a deadline. The sidebar lists what we couldn't confirm.
