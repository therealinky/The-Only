Krea has been shipping its AI generation tools directly into the apps designers already work in, rather than asking you to generate somewhere else and import the result. Plugins for Adobe Photoshop and Adobe After Effects landed in early October 2026, alongside one for Adobe Premiere Pro.

## What changed

The Photoshop plugin, out October 7, brings Krea's models into your layer stack: results come back as ordinary Photoshop layers instead of a separate exported file, and it runs as a desktop plugin for both macOS and Windows. You sign in with a Krea account, and generation happens on Krea's servers.

The After Effects plugin, out October 5, goes further than straight image generation. Krea describes it as letting its Agent create motion graphics, resize video, and handle sound and images, all from inside your composition, and it requires After Effects 2025 or newer. Krea also lists a Premiere Pro plugin, which brings generated results into a project as standard media assets, plus a Blender add-on that shows up as its own tab in the 3D viewport sidebar.

Each plugin needs an internet connection, since the actual generation runs on Krea's servers rather than locally.

## Why it matters for designers

The gap between "a cool AI generation tool" and "a tool that's actually in your workflow" is mostly about friction: how many tabs you switch between, how many times you export and reimport, how much a result has to be massaged before it behaves like the rest of your project. Getting results back as real Photoshop layers or real Premiere media, instead of a flat image you then bring in and reassemble by hand, removes a step that otherwise happens on every single generation.

The After Effects plugin is the more interesting bet of the three. Motion graphics and sound aren't the usual pitch for a still-image generator, and handling them from inside a comp instead of through separate tools is a genuinely different way of working, not just a faster version of the same one.

## What to watch out for

All of this depends on a live connection to Krea's servers, so it's worth checking what that means for working offline, on a locked-down network, or on anything covered by an NDA, since generation isn't happening locally. We haven't tested any of these plugins ourselves yet, so treat the sidebar as open questions, especially around what account data or project content gets sent along with a generation request.
