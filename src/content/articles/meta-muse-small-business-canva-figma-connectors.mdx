Meta's Muse, the personal AI agent it launched in early September, got a business edition on September 29. The part designers should notice: Canva and Figma are both on its list of tools a business owner can connect in a few clicks.

## What changed

Muse for Small Business lets an owner hand the agent a goal and connect the tools they already use so it can work across them. Meta's announcement lists Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, and Zoom, plus the business's own Facebook and Instagram accounts. Businesses can also apply for custom connectors.

For Canva, the pitch from Canva's co-founder in Meta's post is that your templates and tools are right there in the conversation, helping turn an early idea into something on-brand, editable, and ready to share. Meta didn't describe what the Figma connector does specifically, beyond listing it.

Meta's main guardrail is that nothing publishes, sends, or spends without the owner's approval. Muse is free for most uses, with paid plans for heavier use, and it's US and Canada only for now.

## Why it matters for designers

If you design for small businesses, this is the scenario to plan for: a client asks Muse for this week's promo graphics, and Muse reaches into the Canva templates and brand assets you set up for them. That can be a good thing. A well-built template system suddenly gets used more, by the people it was built for.

It also raises the stakes on how those templates are built. Locked brand elements, clear text styles, and sensible placeholder copy are what keep an agent's output on-brand. A loose template that relies on a human knowing which bits not to touch is going to drift.

And for freelancers, it's worth thinking about where the boundary sits. Routine social graphics might move to the client's agent. Brand systems, campaigns, and the templates that make the agent's work look good are still design work, and arguably more valuable now.

## What to watch out for

This is new, and the details are thin. Meta hasn't said what the Canva or Figma connectors can and can't do, or what Muse keeps from connected accounts. Meta's separate Manus agent has had a Canva connector since June with documented abilities, but nobody has said Muse's version works the same way, so don't assume it does.

Muse has also already run into trouble elsewhere. Amazon blocked it from shopping on Amazon.com in late September, saying the agent didn't identify itself and appeared to store customer credentials, claims Meta disputes. That's about shopping, not design tools, but it's a reason to be deliberate about which accounts you connect.

If a client wants to connect a Canva or Figma account you also work in, talk about permissions first. Shared team spaces, client files from other projects, and unreleased work could all be within reach of whatever the agent can see.
