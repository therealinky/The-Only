Kling announced its next video model in late September: Kling 4.0, due in October. The full model isn't out yet, but a lighter version, Kling 4.0 Flash, is already live for Ultra Yearly subscribers.

## What changed

The biggest headline is length. Per Tech360's coverage, Kling 4.0 generates single-pass clips of up to 30 seconds, double the previous 15-second limit, so a full social spot or a longer shot no longer has to be stitched together from separate generations.

The other changes are about control. Kling says the model accepts up to 15 multimodal references (Tech360 breaks that down as a mix of images, video clips, and subjects) and up to 10 keyframes. Kling's own post also highlights more stable motion and high-quality stereo audio, and Tech360 reports multi-language audio support.

On output, Tech360 reports 4K and 1080p, a 21:9 ultra-wide option, and 10-bit HDR, though the HDR part is listed as still pending.

Kling 4.0 Flash is positioned as the faster, cheaper version aimed at high-volume work. It's live now, but only on the Ultra Yearly plan. The full release date hasn't been given beyond "October."

## Why it matters for designers

Clip length and consistency have been the two biggest practical limits on AI video for real production. Thirty seconds in one pass covers a lot of social and ad formats outright, and stitching shorter clips together has always been where characters, lighting, and products drift.

The reference and keyframe controls matter even more for anyone doing brand work. Being able to feed in product shots, a character, and style frames, then pin the action to specific keyframes, moves this closer to directing a shot rather than rolling the dice on a prompt.

## What to watch out for

This is a preview. Most of these specs describe a model that isn't released yet, and the most demanding one, HDR, is already flagged as coming later. Judge it on the actual release, not the announcement.

Early access is limited to Ultra Yearly subscribers, and the Flash version is the lighter model, so early impressions from Flash users may not reflect what the full model does.

Longer clips usually mean more credits per generation. Kling hasn't published pricing for 4.0 in the sources we found, so budget for that before you plan a project around it.
