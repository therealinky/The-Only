Anthropic shipped two things on October 8 that matter for designers. One is brand new: Claude Motion, which builds short animated explainers from a prompt. The other is a graduation: Claude Design, along with Docs and Slides, is out of beta and open to every plan.

## What changed

**Claude Motion** is now in beta on Team and Enterprise plans. You ask Claude for the animation in a regular conversation. The kind of thing Anthropic has in mind is a 30-second explainer for an all-hands, a bit of motion on a chart in a board deck, or a quick product walkthrough for onboarding.

The interesting part is how it works. Anthropic says Motion doesn't use a video generation model. Claude writes code that animates your text, charts, shapes, and images, so there's no generated footage and no AI-generated people. Because it's built from code, you can change any word, number, or timing directly in the editor, or just ask Claude to do it. When it's done, you download an MP4.

From there you can open the animation in another editor. Anthropic lists Adobe, Descript, HeyGen, Higgsfield, invideo, Luma AI, and Runway, with Canva and Captions marked as coming soon. On Adobe's side, the hand-off is a "Send to Adobe" option that opens the animation in the Firefly Video Editor, where you can cut and rearrange it, adjust pacing, refine audio and color, add your own footage to the same timeline, and generate new clips.

On Enterprise, Motion is off by default. An admin has to turn it on under Organization settings, in the Artifacts section.

**Claude Design, Docs, and Slides** are out of beta and available on every plan, including Free. Anthropic says people have made more than 45 million docs, decks, and designs in Claude so far. Alongside that:

- Design has worked inside regular Claude conversations since September 16, not just on its own site, and that's now where it lives.
- PowerPoint and PDF exports keep the editor's formatting, and decks can go to Google Slides as editable files.
- You can edit docs, decks, and designs in the Claude mobile app.
- If your admin allows it, you can share designs, slides, and animations outside your organization or with anyone who has the link.
- For Enterprise, Docs, Slides, and Design switch on by default on October 15, unless an admin turns them on sooner.

## Why it matters for designers

Motion fills a gap a lot of designers know well: someone needs "just a little animation" on a chart or a feature explainer, and it's not worth opening a motion tool for. Since Motion animates the actual elements instead of generating video, the numbers on a chart stay the real numbers and the text stays editable, which is the part generative video usually gets wrong.

The hand-off list is the other tell. Anthropic isn't positioning Motion as a finished-video tool. It's a fast first pass you can finish in an editor you already use, and for Adobe users that means landing on a real timeline instead of re-exporting and re-importing.

Design coming out of beta and onto the Free plan also means more clients and colleagues will have it. Expect more "I mocked something up in Claude" moments in your inbox.

## What to watch out for

If your team uses the standalone Claude Design site, there's a deadline. Anthropic says claude.ai/design stays available until December 14. A "Migrate team design systems" button on the Artifacts page copies every design system in your organization into Claude, and existing projects keep working in the standalone version until it closes. But your chats with Claude and project comments stay behind and won't be available after that date, and public links to standalone projects stop working then too. If you've shared standalone links with clients, plan to replace them.

Motion is a beta, and it's limited to Team and Enterprise. Anthropic doesn't mention usage limits or pricing for it, so don't build a client deliverable schedule around it yet.

And because Motion is code-driven animation of text, shapes, charts, and images, it's not a substitute for footage. If a piece needs real video or a person on screen, that's what the hand-off to an editor is for.
