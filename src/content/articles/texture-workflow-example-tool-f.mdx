Tileable textures are one of the more forgiving generative-AI use cases for 3D work, provided the seams actually tile. Here's the workflow we used with Example Tool F's seamless-tiling mode.

## Why this works

The seamless-tiling mode wraps the generation so opposite edges match, which is the part that's tedious to fix by hand afterward. It doesn't fix everything, but it removes the worst of the manual cleanup.

## Tutorial

1. **Write a material-only prompt** — describe the surface (e.g. "weathered concrete, subtle cracks") without describing a scene or object.
2. **Enable seamless-tiling mode** before generating, not as a post-process step.
3. **Generate at the highest resolution your plan allows**, since downscaling is easier than upscaling later.
4. **Preview the tile at 2×2 before committing** — most tools offer a tiled preview; check it at actual viewing distance, not just zoomed into one tile.
5. **Bring it into your engine or DCC tool** and apply it to a simple plane first to confirm the tiling holds at your intended texel density.
6. **Generate matching normal and roughness maps separately** if your pipeline needs them — this workflow only covers the base color pass.

## Where it breaks down

Directional patterns (grain, brick coursing) sometimes reveal the tile boundary under raking light. Test under your actual lighting setup, not just flat ambient light, before finalizing.
