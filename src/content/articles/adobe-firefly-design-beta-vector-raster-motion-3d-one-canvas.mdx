Adobe opened MAX today with a pile of announcements, and the one most aimed at brand and graphic designers is Firefly Design. It's a new beta workspace inside Adobe Firefly that puts several kinds of work, raster, vector, motion, 3D, and print, onto one canvas. We're reporting this from Adobe's announcements and launch-day coverage. We haven't used it yet.

## What changed

Adobe describes Firefly Design as an all-new design editing space where illustration, image editing, animation, 3D, and print live together. The pitch is that you can take a brand identity, a package, or a campaign from first idea to client review without hopping between apps.

Adobe's Creative Cloud announcement frames it as a collaborative space to create, review, and deliver work in one place, with the control and precision pros expect. That suggests review and handoff are built in, not bolted on, though Adobe hasn't shown exactly how commenting or approvals work.

It's in beta, and Adobe lists it alongside Firefly Graph, a separate beta for building repeatable AI workflows, and Firefly Builder, a natural-language prototyping tool that's only in private beta with a waitlist.

## Why it matters for designers

A lot of brand work is the same idea spread across very different outputs: a logo, a packaging flat, a social animation, a 3D product shot, a print piece. Right now that usually means Illustrator, Photoshop, After Effects, a 3D tool, and InDesign, with exports and re-imports between each one. One canvas that holds all of those, if it works well, cuts out a lot of that shuffling, especially in early concept rounds where you're showing a client the whole system at once.

It's also a signal of where Firefly is heading. Firefly started as an image generator, and with Design, Graph, and Builder it's turning into a place where the actual layout and production happen. If your clients already use Firefly for quick generations, expect them to start asking whether they can review a whole campaign there too.

## What to watch out for

Adobe has shared the idea, not the details. The announcements don't say what file types you can import or export, whether a Firefly Design file opens in Illustrator or InDesign, how deep the vector and print tools go compared to those apps, or whether it's web-only. Until that's clear, keep your master files where they are.

It's a beta, so features and behavior can change, and the cost side is fuzzy. Adobe says its new tools are included with Creative Cloud but doesn't say which plans or how generative credits are counted for the AI parts.
