AI-generated vectors have mostly been a picture of a vector: one flat pile of shapes that looks fine until you try to change anything. Figma's November 5 release notes say its agent now produces illustrations and icons that are built to be edited, restyled, or animated afterward.

## What changed

Figma lists a few specific improvements to what the agent generates:

- **Layers are separate and grouped logically**, so you can find and change one part of an illustration without untangling the rest.
- **Line work uses strokes instead of fills.** That means you can change stroke weight or end caps across an icon without redrawing it, which is exactly what you'd do when fitting a new icon into an existing set.
- **You can attach your own illustrations as a style reference**, and Figma says the agent can use them to make more on-brand components or icons at scale.
- **The cleaner structure is meant for Figma Motion**, so the result can be animated by you or by the agent.

Figma's help article on vectors describes the agent drawing the output live on the canvas from a description, with optional reference images. The same article covers Vectorize, a separate tool for turning a static image, such as a scanned sketch or hand lettering, into vector layers.

## Why it matters for designers

The stroke change is the big one for anyone who maintains an icon set. Icon systems live and die on consistent stroke weights and caps, and a generated icon made of filled outlines can't be tuned to match the rest of the set, so it usually gets redrawn. Real strokes make a generated icon a usable starting point rather than a reference sketch.

Style references matter for illustrators and brand teams. Asking for "something in our style" in words rarely works, and pointing the agent at actual examples is a much more direct way to get spot illustrations or icons that sit alongside existing work.

And grouped layers are what make motion practical. Animating a part of an illustration starts with that part being its own layer.

## What to watch out for

The release notes are short. They don't say which plans get the update, whether it's rolling out gradually, or how many AI credits a generation uses, and the help article only says vector features draw on the credits shared across Figma's AI tools. If you're on a plan with a tight credit allowance, try a small test before generating a whole icon set.

"Easy to edit" is Figma's description, not something we've checked. Before relying on it, look at the actual layer structure: whether strokes are true strokes all the way through, whether paths have far more anchor points than they need, and whether shapes line up with your icon grid. Those are the details that decide whether an icon needs cleanup.

Think about style references, too. Attaching your own work is fine, but attaching someone else's illustrations to get "their" style raises the same rights questions as any style mimicry, and Figma's notes don't address what happens with the files you attach. Check your team's content training setting as well, which the sidebar covers.
