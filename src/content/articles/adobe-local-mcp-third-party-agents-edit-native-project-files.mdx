At MAX on November 10, Adobe previewed something aimed at designers who already live in an AI assistant: local Model Context Protocol (MCP) integrations for four Creative Cloud apps. It's a preview, not something you can use today. This is based on Adobe's announcement and launch-day coverage.

## What changed

Adobe says pros will be able to direct their preferred third-party agents to work locally in Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, and After Effects. Adobe's wording is that because it runs locally on your computer, the agent works directly in your native project files, and your layers, vectors, bins, and sequences stay editable.

TechCrunch describes it as letting chatbots like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini use Adobe features. Adobe's example, per 9to5Mac, is connecting Photoshop to ChatGPT to adapt a campaign from new artwork. The same coverage says the integration works with other connected tools, so an agent could also add files to a Google Drive folder or post an update to Slack.

Availability is early 2027, as a private beta. Interested people can sign up through a link Adobe gave in its announcement.

## Why it matters for designers

The pitch is that you wouldn't have to move work between an assistant and your design app. An agent you already use could do repetitive edits directly in the file, and you'd still have real layers to adjust afterward instead of a flattened export.

That's the practical difference from asking a chatbot for an image: the result lands in the file as editable structure.

## What to watch out for

It's a long way off, and details are thin. TechCrunch notes that Adobe didn't spell out exactly which capabilities are exposed, and quotes an Adobe executive saying the company is still working out where to draw the line. Coverage doesn't give setup requirements, supported agents at launch, or pricing.

Letting an outside agent edit original project files is also a real trust question. Keep versions of anything important, and check what the agent you choose does with your files. The sidebar lists what we couldn't confirm.
