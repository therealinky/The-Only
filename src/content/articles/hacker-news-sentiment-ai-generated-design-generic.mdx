This is a synthesis of public discussion, not a tested or maker-announced fact, and not a survey of all designers. Two Hacker News threads, posted months apart, keep circling the same complaint about AI and design. It's not that the output is bad, it's that so much of it looks the same.

## What designers are saying

One thread started from a type-design studio's post explaining why it avoids AI in its own design and production work: the argument was that understanding how bézier curves actually behave takes hands-on practice, and leaning on AI to skip that step produces weaker typography. That view didn't go unchallenged. A good part of the discussion pushed back, arguing that AI isn't fundamentally different from any other tool designers already depend on, since every tool, including language itself, shapes the output it's used to produce. It was a genuinely split discussion, not a pile-on in either direction.

A second, separate thread a month or so later took a more practical angle: people actually using AI agents for design work, and what goes wrong. The recurring complaint there wasn't craft or authenticity, it was sameness, AI-generated interfaces coming across as artificial and unappealing to real users. The fixes people described converged on the same idea: constrain the model instead of trusting its taste. Suggestions included building a real design system first and pointing an agent at that instead of an open canvas, and stripping visual bloat in favor of a single, deliberately chosen color scheme rather than accepting whatever the model defaults to.

## Why it matters for designers

If you're using an AI agent for actual visual output, both threads land on the same practical takeaway: a defined system beats an open-ended prompt. Whether that's a documented design system, a constrained palette and type scale, or older design theory, the advice that keeps showing up is to narrow the model's choices before asking it to make any.

## What to watch out for

This is two threads on one forum, not a survey of designers broadly, and Hacker News' audience skews toward engineers and technical founders more than working designers, so this reflects one particular, vocal, tech-adjacent corner of the conversation rather than designer sentiment specifically. The disagreement inside the first thread is real too: a meaningful part of the discussion pushed back hard on the idea that AI use is fundamentally different from any other design tool, so "AI output looks generic" isn't a view everyone in these threads actually shares.
