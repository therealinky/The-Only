This is a synthesis of public discussion, not a tested or maker-announced fact. Two Hacker News threads, posted months apart, keep circling the same complaint about AI and design. It's not that the output is bad, it's that so much of it looks the same.

## What people are saying

The first thread started with a type-design studio's post titled "We don't use AI in any of our design or production processes." The author was careful to frame it narrowly: "I'm not a die-hard AI hater, just opposed to careless use of it within this narrow field." Their argument was that understanding how bézier curves actually behave takes hands-on practice, and leaning on AI to skip that step produces weaker typography. A commenter, My_Name, extended the point further, arguing that art needing to resonate emotionally requires real human experience behind it, not just output that looks plausible.

That view didn't go unchallenged. Commenter combray asked why AI gets treated as fundamentally different from any other tool designers already depend on, and Levitz made a similar point, that every tool, including language itself, shapes the output, not just AI specifically. It was a genuinely split thread, not a pile-on in either direction.

A second, separate thread a month or so later, "How I Design with AI," took a more practical angle: people actually using AI agents for design work, and what goes wrong. The recurring complaint there wasn't craft or authenticity, it was sameness. One commenter, Vishal_Max, said AI-generated websites often come across as artificial and unappealing to real users. The fixes people described in the comments converged on the same idea: constrain the model instead of trusting its taste. jamesponddotco suggested hiring a designer to build a real design system first, then pointing Claude at it rather than an open canvas. rfgplk suggested stripping visual bloat and iterating on a single, deliberately chosen color scheme instead of accepting whatever the model defaults to.

## Why it matters for designers

If you're using an AI agent, Claude or otherwise, for actual visual output, both threads land on the same practical takeaway: a defined system beats an open-ended prompt. Whether that's a documented design system, a constrained palette and type scale, or older design theory (one commenter pointed to Christopher Alexander's "Notes on the Synthesis of Form"), the advice that keeps showing up is to narrow the model's choices before asking it to make any.

## What to watch out for

This is two threads on one forum, not a survey of designers broadly, and Hacker News' audience skews toward engineers and technical founders more than working designers, so treat this as one particular, vocal corner of the conversation rather than a consensus. The disagreement inside the first thread is real too: several commenters pushed back hard on the idea that AI use is fundamentally different from any other design tool, so "AI output looks generic" isn't a view everyone in these threads actually shares.
