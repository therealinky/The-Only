Every campaign has the same unglamorous back half. Once the hero ad is approved, someone has to rebuild it for stories, feeds, banners, and every market it runs in. Over the past week, two AI video companies shipped tools aimed squarely at that job, one for static ads and one for video.

## What changed

**Luma Variants** (Luma calls it Ad Variants in the app) went live on October 1. You upload one approved static ad, pick the formats and languages you need, and it builds a version for every combination. Luma says your logo, headline, and CTA stay intact while the layout and copy adapt to each placement and market.

It covers five standard placements: Story 9:16, Portrait 4:5, Square 1:1, Medium Rectangle 6:5, and Widescreen 16:9. You preview the results, regenerate any version that isn't right, then download the finals or send them to a Luma Canvas board for the team to review. It's in the Discover tab as the Ad Variants card. Luma says this first release covers static ads, resizing, and translation, with more planned.

**Higgsfield's Ad Multiplier** arrived on October 6 inside its Ads Studio, and it works on video. You give it a reference video ad between 4 and 30 seconds long, plus up to 20 images, and it recreates the ad with your own characters, products, and locations while keeping the original's structure. You can swap the product, cast, location, script, hook, or CTA, and Higgsfield says you can try as many hooks and CTAs as you want without re-uploading the reference. Output comes in 480p, 720p, or 1080p.

## Why it matters for designers

Resizing is the part of campaign work almost nobody enjoys and almost every brand designer does. A tool that handles the first pass of a dozen placement and language versions could free up real hours, as long as the result still looks like your layout and not a generic reflow of it.

The two tools solve different halves of the problem. Luma's is about adapting something you already made, faithfully, across sizes and markets. Higgsfield's is about producing new versions of a video ad, so it's closer to concepting than to production cleanup. If you test hooks and CTAs for social video, that's a real shift in how many options you can put in front of a client.

## What to watch out for

"Stays intact" is the promise to test. Reflowing a layout from 16:9 to 9:16 is exactly where type sizes, safe zones, and spacing tend to break, and a translated headline can run much longer than the original. Check every version at actual size before it ships, and have someone who reads the language check the translation.

Higgsfield's tool recreates a reference ad's structure, which raises an obvious question: whose ad is the reference? Using your own past spots is one thing. Rebuilding a competitor's ad with your product swapped in is a rights and reputation problem the tool won't stop you from creating.

Neither announcement says anything about pricing, data handling, or labeling, so those are all open questions in the sidebar.
