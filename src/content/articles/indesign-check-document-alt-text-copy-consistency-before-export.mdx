InDesign 22.0, announced on November 10, adds a panel called Check Document. It scans a layout for accessibility gaps and copy problems in one pass, and you can run it whenever you like or have it check as part of exporting. We haven't tried it ourselves, so this is based on Adobe's own announcement posts and the early replies under them.

## What changed

Check Document looks for four kinds of problems. On the accessibility side, it flags images that don't have alt text and tables that don't have a summary. On the copy side, it catches inconsistent formatting, such as mixed date styles, unit names, URL styles, or spellings, plus grammar issues.

You open it from File or Window, or let it run during export. Results show up as cards, one per kind of problem, and clicking an instance jumps you to it in the layout. From there you can apply the suggestion, dismiss it, or fix every instance on a card at once. For consistency problems, it recommends one format and lets you choose which one to apply throughout. For alt text and table summaries, it can generate a draft that you accept, regenerate, or edit.

The same release adds an AI Assistant in beta, which takes plain-language requests to edit content, layouts, and objects. Adobe says it runs on partner models, including GPT 5.2 and Claude Sonnet 4.6.

## Why it matters for designers

Alt text and table summaries are the parts of an accessible PDF that tend to get skipped when a deadline hits, mostly because there's no single place that tells you what's missing. A panel that lists every untagged image before export makes that gap visible, even if you end up writing the descriptions yourself.

The consistency check is a quieter win for anyone laying out long documents written by several people. Catching "Nov. 3" next to "3 November," or "colour" next to "color," usually falls to a proofreader late in the process. Having it surface while you're still in the layout saves a round of corrections.

## What to watch out for

Read every generated description before you accept it. Good alt text depends on why an image is on the page, and a generated draft can only describe what's in it. The same goes for table summaries.

The early replies are mixed. Two people on business accounts said the panel didn't appear for them or their teammates. Another got it working by reinstalling. Two people reported Interactive PDF exports crashing after the update, one of them right after opening Check Document, though a community expert couldn't reproduce it on their Mac. One also said the automatic check at export kept switching itself back on. Another said a scan found nothing in a file they'd deliberately broken. A few replies also pushed back on paying a credit to apply a correction. Treat it as a helpful first pass rather than a guarantee, and keep your current accessibility checks in place until you've seen how it handles your own files.

If you're on Creative Cloud Standard, note that alt text costs a credit per image, which adds up quickly on an image-heavy catalog or annual report. The sidebar has the full breakdown.
