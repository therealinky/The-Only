Culling is the part of a shoot nobody gets into photography for. ON1 Photo RAW 2027 is now available on Mac and Windows, and its headline AI addition is a Culling Assistant meant to take the first pass off your hands.

ON1 announced the release in September and said it would ship in early November. Its press release and blog post were both updated this week to say it's out.

## What changed

**An AI Culling Assistant.** According to ON1, it groups similar shots, flags frames where someone's eyes are closed, and flags out-of-focus subjects, so you can get through large batches faster. ON1's product page says it sorts a shoot in minutes. It's only in Photo RAW MAX 2027, not the standard edition.

**A rebuilt RAW engine.** ON1 says the new engine gives sharper detail, richer color, and more natural shadows and highlights.

**New NoNoise AI models.** There are new noise reduction models for wildlife, portraits, astrophotography, and scanned film, and a sharper version of ON1's Tack Sharp de-blurring for soft or slightly missed shots.

Smaller things ride along too: improved Brilliance AI models for automatic enhancement, faster AI tools (ON1 calls out Windows in particular), and optional cloud rendering for Generative Erase and Crop if you're on MAX. The mobile app is due a few weeks after the desktop release.

## Why it matters for designers

If you shoot your own product photos, events, or brand work, culling is often the slowest step, and it's pure sorting rather than creative work. Having the software group the near-duplicates and point at the blinks and soft frames gets you to the interesting decisions faster.

The other draw is the license. ON1 still sells Photo RAW as a one-time purchase, which matters to anyone tired of stacking subscriptions. Just note that the culling feature is in MAX, not the base version.

## What to watch out for

ON1 describes the Culling Assistant in fairly broad strokes. It doesn't say how it decides a frame is out of focus, whether you can tune how strict it is, or whether it picks a best shot from each group or just groups them. So treat it as a first pass and review the flagged frames yourself, especially if you shoot with shallow depth of field on purpose, where "soft" can be the point.

On privacy, ON1 says most of its AI tools run on your computer, but it doesn't say whether culling is one of them. If you're sorting client shoots under an NDA, that's worth confirming first.

Pricing takes a little care too. The product page lists the standard edition and the MAX subscription clearly, but shows more than one price for the MAX one-time license during the launch period. Check what you'll actually pay at checkout, and sign in if you're upgrading.
