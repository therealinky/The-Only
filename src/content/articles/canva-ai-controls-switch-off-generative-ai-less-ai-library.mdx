If you've been wishing Canva would let you just say no to its AI, it now does. On November 19, Canva launched two AI Controls on Free and Pro: a switch that hides generative AI features across the app, and an option to see less AI-generated content when you browse the Canva library.

## What changed

The first setting is the **Generative AI Control**. Turn it off and Canva's generative features are hidden across the app. You can turn it back on whenever you like. It lives under Profile > Settings > Permissions. Canva's help page lists 21 features it hides, including Canva AI, Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Media, Magic Edit, Magic Layers, Dream Lab, Canva Code, AI Shapes, AI Charts, Translate designs, and Image to Video. New generative features will follow the setting as they're added.

The second is the **AI-Generated Content Control**. Canva says a small part of its library is AI-generated, things like abstract backgrounds and illustrated graphics, and those assets are tagged as AI-generated in their metadata. The toggle, labeled AI-generated content, is on by default. Turn it off and fewer of those assets show up in search results and recommendations, so more work by human creators comes to the top. It doesn't change any designs you've already made. You'll find it under Profile > Your account > AI Personalization.

Canva is rolling both settings out gradually, so you may not see them yet.

Some tools stay put either way. Canva calls Background Remover and Magic Grab "assistive" because they edit what's already there rather than generate something new. They keep working with generative AI switched off, and using them won't add the AI-generated label to your design's metadata. Canva points out that this matters for social platforms that read that metadata: a quick background removal shouldn't get your post tagged as AI-made.

## Why it matters for designers

Earlier this month we wrote about a steady stream of threads on the Figma and Adobe forums that came down to the same request: give us a real way to turn this off. Canva has now shipped the kind of single, app-wide switch those threads were asking for, and it's on the free plan, not tucked behind a subscription.

It's useful even if you do use AI sometimes. If a client has asked for no generative AI in their work, you can switch it off for that stretch so nothing slips in by accident, then turn it back on later. And the library filter is a practical help for illustrators and brand designers who want to start from creator-made templates and graphics, which Canva notes pay their creators each time they're used.

The split between generative and assistive tools is the part to understand. Canva is drawing a clear line: tools that make something new are behind the switch and get labeled, while tools that cut out or move what you already have aren't and don't. That's a sensible line, but it's Canva's line, and it may not match a client's or a platform's definition of "AI-assisted."

## What to watch out for

The switch hides more than the obvious image tools. Canva's help page lists things like AI Charts, Translate designs, and Auto-match in Bulk Create alongside the headline features, so check the full list before you rely on one of them with generative AI off.

The library option reduces AI content rather than promising to remove it. Canva says some AI-generated templates and elements may still appear. It also doesn't affect Brand Templates or searches of your own uploads, and it doesn't apply inside Canva AI, third-party apps, or when you use Canva through its API or MCP server. If a project needs to be entirely human-made, check each asset rather than relying on the filter.

On team plans, these settings don't exist. Canva's help page says they're only on Free and Pro, and that on Teams, Business, Enterprise, and Education your admin controls AI access instead. The generative AI setting also doesn't carry over if your account moves between those plans and Free or Pro. Affinity, which Canva also owns, isn't mentioned. And training is handled by a separate privacy setting for AI feature improvement, so turning these switches off doesn't change that on its own.
