If you've been wishing Canva would let you just say no to its AI, it now does. On November 19, Canva launched two AI Controls on Free and Pro: a switch that hides generative AI features across the app, and an option to see less AI-generated content when you browse the Canva library.

## What changed

The first setting is the **Generative AI Control**. Turn it off and Canva's generative features are hidden across the app. You can turn it back on whenever you like. It lives under Profile > Settings > Permissions.

The second is the **AI-Generated Content Control**. Canva says a small part of its library is AI-generated, things like abstract backgrounds and illustrated graphics, and those assets are tagged as AI-generated in their metadata. With this option on, fewer of them show up in search results and recommendations, so more work by human creators comes to the top. It doesn't change any designs you've already made. You'll find it under Profile > Your account > AI Personalization.

Some tools stay put either way. Canva calls Background Remover and Magic Grab "assistive" because they edit what's already there rather than generate something new. They keep working with generative AI switched off, and using them won't add the AI-generated label to your design's metadata. Canva points out that this matters for social platforms that read that metadata: a quick background removal shouldn't get your post tagged as AI-made.

## Why it matters for designers

Earlier this month, a steady stream of threads on the Figma and Adobe forums came down to the same request: give us a real way to turn this off. Canva has now shipped the kind of single, app-wide switch those threads were asking for, and it's on the free plan, not tucked behind a subscription.

It's useful even if you do use AI sometimes. If a client has asked for no generative AI in their work, you can switch it off for that stretch so nothing slips in by accident, then turn it back on later. And the library filter is a practical help for illustrators and brand designers who want to start from creator-made templates and graphics, which Canva notes pay their creators each time they're used.

The split between generative and assistive tools is the part to understand. Canva is drawing a clear line: tools that make something new are behind the switch and get labeled, while tools that cut out or move what you already have aren't and don't. That's a sensible line, but it's Canva's line, and it may not match a client's or a platform's definition of "AI-assisted."

## What to watch out for

The announcement doesn't list which features the switch hides. It names Magic Expand and Canva's Craft image model as examples of its AI tools, and Background Remover and Magic Grab as the ones that stay, but there's no full list. After you flip it, look around the tools you normally use to see what's gone.

The library option reduces AI content rather than promising to remove it. If a project needs to be entirely human-made, check each asset rather than relying on the filter.

It's also unclear how this works on team plans. Canva says the new controls join AI permissions that admins already have on Business, Enterprise, Teams, and Education, but it doesn't say whether the new personal switch shows up on those plans or whether an admin can turn it on for everyone. Affinity, which Canva also owns, isn't mentioned, and neither is whether turning AI off changes how your content is used for training. If those questions matter to you, they need a separate check.
