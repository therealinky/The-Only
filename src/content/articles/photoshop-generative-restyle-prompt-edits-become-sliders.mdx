Adobe announced at MAX today that Photoshop's Generative Restyle is leaving beta. It's one of the quieter launches, but it fixes something that makes prompt-based edits hard to use on real jobs: you only ever got one strength. This is based on Adobe's announcement and launch-day coverage. We haven't tried the release version ourselves.

## What changed

You type a prompt describing a new look for an image, and Photoshop generates it. Adobe's announcement says Generative Restyle turns those prompt-based edits into adjustable sliders. Launch coverage adds that the result sits on its own editable layer, so you can come back later and change it, and that the slider lets you make the effect stronger or weaker without writing a new prompt.

Adobe's demo, as DPReview describes it, turned a midday landscape into a golden-hour scene with a pastel sunset, then used the slider to move through variations of the effect. TNW's coverage shows a moodier example, a car on a rainy night street pushed toward a noir look.

Relight, the virtual studio lights tool we covered in beta last month, is leaving beta at the same time. DPReview says it adds up to three virtual lights, and like Restyle, the effect sits on its own layer you can revise. The two do different jobs: Relight changes where the light comes from, while Restyle changes the overall look of the whole image.

## Why it matters for designers

The usual problem with prompt edits is that they're all or nothing. The model either overshoots, and a subtle grade becomes a filter, or you rewrite the prompt again and again hoping for something in between. A slider on a separate layer makes it work more like an adjustment layer: generate once, then dial it in, compare, and adjust again after client feedback.

That's handy for mood exploration on a shoot, matching a set of photos to a campaign look, or showing a client three intensities of the same idea without three separate generations.

## What to watch out for

None of the coverage says how many generative credits a restyle uses, or whether sliding back and forth triggers new generations. The announcements also don't say how well it holds detail in faces, text, or products, which matters for anything a client has to approve as accurate.

A restyle can change the time of day, the weather, and the feel of a scene. For editorial, documentary, or product photography where the image is meant to show what was really there, that's a bigger edit than a color grade, and it may need disclosure. The sidebar lists what we couldn't confirm.
